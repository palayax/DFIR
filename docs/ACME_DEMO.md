# ACME.Corp — business-risk mock dataset

**Synthetic data only.** ACME.Corp is a fictional company. All DNS names use the
RFC 2606 reserved `.example` TLD. Public IP addresses come from the RFC 5737
documentation ranges and internal ones from RFC 1918. Vulnerability identifiers
are fictional (`ACME-VULN-*`), never real CVE ids.

- Generated by `scripts/gen-acme-dataset.mjs` (seeded PRNG, fixed dates, sorted keys).
- Committed as `web/demo/acme/context.js`.
- Regenerate with `node scripts/gen-acme-dataset.mjs`.
- `--check` fails (exit 1) if the committed file is stale. It runs in
  `build/build.ps1`, in the Pages workflow and in `web/tests/business.test.mjs`.

## The company

- **Profile:** a 500-employee cybersecurity firm with $120M revenue and 340 customers.
- **Business lines:** managed detection & response (MDR), a threat-intelligence SaaS platform, and professional services.
- **Sites:** Boston (HQ, NA SOC), Dublin (EU hub, EU SOC), Singapore (APAC SOC), Tel Aviv (R&D, CI/CD), plus remote staff.
- **Infrastructure:** AWS, Azure and GCP; a mature CI/CD pipeline (self-hosted runners, artifact repository, secrets manager, GitOps, code signing); a mature BI stack (data warehouse and Power BI).
- **GRC:** ISO 27001:2022, SOC 2 Type II, NIST CSF 2.0, GDPR, NIS2 and PCI DSS 4.0.

## The story the data tells

- **MDR, the largest revenue line:** at risk through the SOC tooling. A VPN
  appliance is missing a vendor hotfix, the SOAR service account holds
  domain-admin privileges, EDR tamper protection is off on the SOC jump hosts,
  one jump host contacted infrastructure on a threat-intel blocklist, and an
  unverified OAuth app was granted mailbox consent. The MTTD/MTTR SLAs and
  platform availability come under pressure.
- **Customer Portal:** exposed through a known-exploited API framework flaw, a
  WAF in detection-only mode, a publicly listable export bucket and an
  internet-reachable database admin endpoint. This threatens the 99.95%
  availability SLA and customer PII under GDPR.
- **CI/CD:** an over-privileged CI token, shared self-hosted runners, unsigned
  artifacts and secrets in repository history threaten release KPIs, the EU
  sovereign-region goal, and supply-chain integrity.
- **Identity:** missing MFA on privileged accounts and slow leaver
  de-provisioning. Every service depends on identity, so this flows into all of them.
- **Missing KPIs:** BI & Decision Support and Identity & Access have no KPIs from
  their owners. The AI-proposed KPIs (with rationale) fill the gap.
- **Trend:** total value at risk rose sharply in Q3 2026 and now sits at about
  twice the board appetite. Fixing the top five risks brings it down by about $3.3M.
- **Denial of service:** the website, portal and TIP API edges have no
  volumetric DDoS protection, the website origin is reachable around the CDN,
  the TIP API has no per-client rate limits, and the SOC saw three request-rate
  episodes against the portal login in September (hunt HUNT-01).

## Real products and frameworks

- **Products.** Assets carry a real vendor/product (Okta, GitHub Enterprise, Salesforce,
  Microsoft 365, Splunk, CrowdStrike Falcon, Cloudflare, Palo Alto Networks, Cisco,
  Qualys, Veeam, AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, Kubernetes, Claude and others). These
  are rendered as vendored Simple Icons logos or brand-coloured text badges.
- **Fictional flaws stay unbranded.** Every vulnerability id is fictional
  (`ACME-VULN-*`). Assets that carry a fictional product *flaw* (the VPN
  appliance, the portal API framework) are deliberately left unbranded, so no
  finding reads as a claim about a real product.
- **Control crosswalk.** Controls map to ISO 27001:2022, SOC 2, NIST CSF 2.0,
  NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5, FedRAMP Moderate, CIS Controls v8, GDPR, NIS2 and
  PCI DSS 4.0.
- **Finding references.** Findings cite MITRE ATT&CK / ATLAS techniques (official
  names and links) and OWASP Top 10 (2021), OWASP API Top 10 (2023) or OWASP LLM
  Top 10 (2025) entries.
- **Framework catalogue.** `framework_catalog` lists each framework's issuer and
  official URL.

## The BIA hierarchy (seven levels)

The BIA explorer (`#/bia`) walks from business SLOs down to evidence:

| Level | What it is | ACME.Corp examples |
|---|---|---|
| L1 SLO | Business objective with a financial basis, plan forecast and target | ARR $150M, gross revenue retention 95%, customer SLA attainment 99.5% |
| L2 Policy | What must happen, and when, for the SLOs to hold (weighted share of each SLO) | "Keep customer-facing platforms available at least 99.95% each month" |
| L3 Business process | The procedure that implements policies | 24x7 alert triage & escalation; contract renewal & QBR |
| L4 Component | People, procedures, data and systems a process needs | CISO; release signing; customer PII; company website |
| L5 Cyber-risk scenario | What can go wrong with a component | Denial of service; ransomware; account takeover |
| L6 Hunt / finding | IOC/IOA hunts and posture findings the scenario is derived from | HUNT-01 edge request-rate anomalies; CR-070 no DDoS protection |
| L7 Evidence | Raw records behind findings and hunts | CDN metric series, config snapshots, scanner results, tickets |

Every link is many-to-many: the CISO is a component of six processes, "Denial of
service" applies to five systems, and a policy protects up to six SLOs.
Components attach to the estate by asset id or by an attribute query (for
example, every endpoint in the Finance department), so findings on those assets
reach the component automatically.

With every mitigation off, the computed picture is:
- ARR is projected to miss target ($4.8M shortfall, 3.2%; score 85, critical).
- Customer SLA attainment and gross retention are projected to breach.
- Net promoter score stays on track (score 33).

Ransomware, data breach and denial of service carry most of the shortfall.

Cyber findings are deliberately at **risk-register level**, phrased the way a
vulnerability scanner, a CSPM, an identity-posture tool or a SOC would list
them, with ATT&CK technique ids as labels. The dataset contains no attack
telemetry or procedures.

## Demo mode

The header's **Demo** button runs three guided stories on this dataset:

1. **New high-risk data source.** The preset is "mft-gw01, partner
   file-transfer gateway": internet-facing, a known-exploited critical
   vulnerability, an exposed admin interface, a shared admin without MFA,
   credentials on a darknet market, and no EDR.
   - ARR shortfall $4.8M → $5.6M (score 85 → 89).
   - Net revenue retention goes from at risk to breach projected.
   - The gateway becomes the #1 root-cause asset for ARR.
2. **Mitigate an existing asset.** MIT-05 (phishing-resistant MFA) on Okta, the
   largest existing root cause, takes ARR from $5.6M → $4.9M after story 1.
3. **Change the business context.**
   - The ARR plan forecast goes from $152.5M to $157M, so ARR comes back on track
     (score 86 → 50).
   - The SLA target goes from 99.5% to 99.7%, so the SLA score goes 76 → 99.
